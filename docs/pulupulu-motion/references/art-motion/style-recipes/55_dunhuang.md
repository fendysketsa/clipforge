## Scope

Pick a period first and work from that; do not blend Northern Dynasties, High Tang and Late Tang into one "Dunhuang style":
- Northern Dynasties (Cave 257, the Deer King Jataka; Cave 285, Western Wei flying apsaras; Cave 428, Northern Zhou): earth-red ground, white beasts, serrated mountains, blue-green wind leaves, apsaras flying sideways. v1 of this card does this branch.
- High Tang (Cave 112, pipa played behind the back; Cave 45, bodhisattvas): all-over gold and azurite, plump figures, ensemble music and dance. Not done yet.

## Stable Features (Observed in the Originals)

- Ground: 257 is a whole surface of earth red of the #92342d kind; 285 is creamy white #e8dcc5; the two can be split into upper and lower registers with a multicolour horizontal band between them (black / white / blue / ochre) plus bead roundels.
- Form: the beast is one solid white block with a single contour line and almost no interior lines; mountains are rows of narrow tall serrated triangles, each in one colour with one side darkened, and large areas of red ground show between mountain and mountain; the figures are small and spread horizontally.
- Apsaras (Cave 285): fly horizontally, head first, scarves streaming backwards, the lower body turning into one long skirt trailing behind; small face, fine long brows and eyes, red flush on the cheeks; lines are red-brown iron-wire / orchid-leaf strokes.
- Wind leaves: comma-shaped blue-green curling leaves, turning backwards in groups.
- Colour: azurite #3e919c, malachite #577f7a, vermilion, ochre, lead white; lead white darkens with age through oxidation, and the skin tones in Cave 428 have gone grey-blue.
- Wall surface: crazing, flaking that exposes the ground layer, salt efflorescence turning white, mud-plaster grain; cracks run across the people and beasts in the painting. A lozenge band along the top, a hanging-curtain triangle band along the bottom.

## Distinction from Adjacent Styles

Not Persian miniature (all-over, precise, gold lines); not Giotto (the lime white of fresco plaster); not guofeng illustration (that is glossy gradients and cloud motifs). Dunhuang's texture is "dry, old, cracked"; its colour is mineral, not paint.

## Production Route (The One v1 Used)

`scenes/55_dunhuang.js`: wall ground (P.texture mud) → two registers + horizontal band + top lozenges + bottom hanging curtain (cached) → serrated mountains (cached) → motion: wind leaves turning left in layers by speed, apsaras (`apsara()`, widening ribbons `ribbon()`), scattered flowers, white deer running (`deer()`, two beats of airborne–gathered), hoof dust → overlay (crazing, flaking, salt, over the painting) → mud-plaster grain multiplied.

## v1 Production Run (2026-10-16, self-score ~72, taste items pending author)

- Source anchoring: Cave 257 Deer King Jataka, Cave 285 Western Wei apsaras, Cave 428 Northern Zhou apsaras, Cave 112 pipa played behind the back (sources in the reference.md generated by fetch_reference; the Cave 112 and Cave 257 images are photographic collages, so only the palette and composition were taken, not traced).
- Motion: wind leaves turning back, apsaras bobbing up and down with ribbons swaying, scattered flowers falling, white deer and fawns running, hoof dust, drifting dust.
- Known gaps: mountains are still too geometric-triangular, missing the layered compression rhythm of Cave 257; apsara torsos are flat, missing the three-quarter volume; lead-white oxidation darkening not done; the High Tang branch not done.
- Not yet verified: cross-subject (replacing the apsara with another subject), vertical format, compositing with a voice-over film.
